# Book-Reading Series Contract

## Series Promise

Produce an editorial documentary animation that helps a general viewer understand one consequential idea from a book. Establish trust with representative real evidence, expose the book's mechanism through authored motion, and carry the argument with natural Mandarin performance.

The result must not feel like a slideshow, a sequence of posters, an audiobook with filler footage, or a book-summary template.

## Locked Defaults

| Dimension | Series default |
|---|---|
| Delivery | Native `1440x1080`, `4:3`, `30fps` |
| Mobile intent | Larger phone viewing area than 16:9 while retaining horizontal explanation space |
| Design read | Cold-white, lucid, contemporary editorial documentary |
| Composition | Real evidence as subject; local graphics as explanatory mechanism |
| Authoring | Bespoke/Atelier for a hero episode; no stock creative scene catalog |
| Motion grammar | One semantic verb and one visible result per scene |
| Motion phases | `setup -> action -> reaction -> result` |
| Pace | Natural `1.0x`; quick confirmations, measured mechanisms, deliberate conclusions |
| Narration | Local approved Mandarin profile when available; continuous thought units |
| Opening | Full book cover; spoken title followed by about one second of hold |
| Subtitles | Complete, mathematically centered, separated from dark/busy evidence |
| Materials | Representative, traceable, rights-aware, processed before use |
| Music | Explicit decision; restrained documentary bed with dialogue ducking when approved |
| Revision | Freeze accepted layers; patch the smallest affected surface |
| Review | Playable evidence first; only necessary objective QA |

These are defaults, not permission to bypass proposal decisions. Runtime, provider, model, music source, paid calls, and major creative changes still follow the governance and approval rules in `AGENT_GUIDE.md`.

## Episode-Specific Work

Redesign these for every book:

- the central thesis and counterintuitive opening;
- the audience question and narrative progression;
- the representative evidence list;
- the meaning and treatment of every source image;
- each scene's physical metaphor and semantic verb;
- layouts, transitions, camera targets, and visual bridges;
- the use of accent color and any signature device;
- narration performance beats and difficult pronunciations;
- the music track and the final conclusion.

Do not copy a previous episode's finished visual answer. A repeated mechanism is allowed only when the new argument genuinely contains that mechanism and the repetition is intentional.

## Content Standard

Open with the book and its most useful claim. Surface an apparent contradiction or counterintuitive proposition early. Explain causes and incentives using concrete examples, then land on a practical lens the viewer can reuse.

Prefer this progression when it fits the book:

1. Name the book and thesis.
2. Challenge the common impression.
3. Show the concrete system or dilemma.
4. Explain how the mechanism works.
5. Show why it once worked or remains attractive.
6. Reveal costs, limits, or second-order effects.
7. Return a reusable question or mental model to the viewer.

Do not force the structure when the book requires another one, but preserve the hook, mechanism, consequence, and landing.

## Evidence Standard

Before searching, define the exact function of each source slot. A source must identify a relevant person, institution, place, object, document, event, or measurable condition. Generic streets, skylines, office workers, and anonymous buildings are not adequate substitutes.

For every external asset, record at least:

- scene and claim served;
- creator or institution;
- title or description;
- date when available;
- original source page;
- rights or license status;
- access date;
- original local path and derived local path;
- deterministic processing performed;
- file hash.

When evidence leads a scene, keep it large enough to inspect. Preserve legible names, dates, titles, and relevant details. Prefer a localized zoom over retyping an official document into a fake screenshot.

## Visual Standard

Use cold white, near-black, steel blue-gray, restrained causal red, and rare warning amber. Preserve the reality of photographs; use light desaturation and contrast unification, not a blanket duotone or vintage filter.

Graphics must attach to evidence and reveal relationships. If the graphic layer can stand alone after the evidence is removed, reconsider whether the real material is merely decorative. If the evidence remains unchanged while labels float over it, reconsider whether the animation explains anything.

Use composition variation across full evidence, split evidence, document inspection, map or ledger space, close detail, and restrained conceptual transformation. Keep one primary causal chain and no more than a few supporting labels in a frame.

## Motion Standard

Every scene declares:

- viewer intention;
- primary subject;
- one semantic verb;
- setup state;
- action;
- reaction;
- visible result;
- camera purpose;
- exit or bridge;
- intentional hold;
- text and subject occlusion risk.

Do not stretch a single entrance across the length of a spoken sentence. Use short, readable action windows, then hold the result. Vary timing by meaning: a stamp may hit quickly; a causal chain needs time to unfold; a conclusion needs time to settle.

## Voice and Audio Standard

Direct the voice as an informed person explaining an idea, not as a formal newsreader and not as a dry TTS read. Use continuous rhetorical units, audible contrast, controlled energy, and deliberate landings. Keep the overall tempo at natural `1.0x` unless the user explicitly changes it.

Use actual provider input punctuation and segmentation to shape delivery. Keep natural trailing energy. Never cut a high-energy syllable directly into digital silence. Inspect boundaries that coincide with scene changes.

When music is included, it must be audible during pauses but make room for speech. Use sidechain ducking with a gentle release so the bed does not pump. Treat prior episode loudness values as references, not universal constants.

## Prohibited Outcomes

Reject an episode when it relies on any of the following:

- traditional presentation slides or centered title cards as the dominant language;
- a sequence of attractive but static posters;
- unprocessed downloaded images pasted into rectangles;
- ordinary streets used as evidence for a specific institution or mechanism;
- black text over dark photographic material;
- decorative lines, particles, or camera drift without meaning;
- repeated fade, slide, zoom, and reveal as the complete motion vocabulary;
- a 16:9 render cropped or squeezed into 4:3;
- a partially cropped opening book cover;
- subtitles aligned by eye instead of the actual frame center;
- narration synthesized sentence by sentence with repeated restarts;
- abrupt digital silence at spoken boundaries;
- full rerenders for a local mix or tail problem;
- redundant QA that delays a result without changing a decision.

## Change Control

For every revision, state:

1. the observed problem;
2. the layer responsible;
3. the smallest planned change;
4. the layers that remain frozen;
5. the evidence that will show the fix worked.

Append changed production decisions to the project's decision log. Never silently mutate a provider, runtime, voice, music source, or approved creative direction.
